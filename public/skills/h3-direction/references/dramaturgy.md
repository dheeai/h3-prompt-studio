## Scene Formula (The Mandatory Foundation)

A scene requires all five elements or it collapses into decoration:

```
Scene = hero's desire + obstacle + space geometry + controlled gaze + editing rhythm
```

**Before writing:** Name each element in one sentence. If you cannot, the scene
isn't ready.

---

## The Three-Detail Rule (Combat Laziness)

Every shot must embed three concrete physical facts:

1. **Environmental pressure** — a spatial fact carrying emotion (flickering
   light, wet asphalt, steam, rain on glass, tight corridor, mirror reflection)
2. **Physical micro-action** — emotion rendered on the actor's body (jaw locks,
   knuckles whiten, lips press flat, eyes drop, fingers curl)
3. **Sound anchor or visual motif** — a recurring perceptual hook tied to the
   piece (stomach growl, reflection in glass, musical sting, clock ticking,
   footsteps)

**Banned placeholder words:** "cinematic," "professional," "high quality,"
"stunning," "epic," "he is sad," "she is angry" — replace with concrete facts.

---

## The Three-Jobs Rule

Every shot must accomplish at least one task:

- **Change emotion** (in hero, viewer, or dynamic between characters)
- **Advance action** (new physical event, information, position)
- **Increase pressure** (stakes rise, clock ticks, space tightens, witness appears)

If a shot does none of these, delete it.

---

## Walter Murch Rule of Six (Editing Priority)

Where to cut, ranked by weight:

1. **Emotion (51%)** — does the cut honor emotional truth?
2. **Story (23%)** — does it advance story or reveal character?
3. **Rhythm (10%)** — does it fall on a musical beat?
4. **Eye-trace (7%)** — does the viewer's gaze receive the new shot naturally?
5. **2D plane (5%)** — respect screen direction axis?
6. **3D space (4%)** — respect location geometry?

> *dhee note:* criterion 3's phrasing ("musical beat") is exactly the vocabulary
> that leaks a score into an H3 render. See `../SKILL.md` §7, Trap 1.

## Blocking as Choreography of Desire

For every character, identify:

- What they want now
- Whom/what they move toward
- Whom/what they move away from
- Whom they corner
- To whom they yield space
- What gesture reveals hidden desire

**Bad:** "He stands near the window."
**Good:** "He edges toward the window but shoulders back toward her, as if
conversation still holds him."

## Staging Controls Subtext

Power signals before dialogue:

- Standing dominates seated
- Character in doorway controls room
- Behind glass = psychological distance
- Shadow = threat or grief
- Negative space = isolation
- Tight framing = suffocation
- Shared frame without eye contact = broken intimacy

**Before writing:** name the power dynamic the staging reveals.

---

## Camera Must Have a Reason

Every camera movement answers "what changed?"

Valid reasons:

- Character made a decision
- New information entered the frame
- Pressure escalated (camera tightens)
- Character looked (camera reveals what they saw)
- Gesture pulled focus (rack focus)
- Space changed (door opened, person entered)

**Bad:** "Cinematic gliding camera movement."
**Good:** "Push-in starts on 'I don't know' and stops on her jaw locking."

---

## Spatial Clarity Beats Montage Hysteria

Viewer must always know:

- Where the hero is
- Where the threat is
- Which direction is escape
- Which direction is decision

**Before fast-cut sequences:** sketch geography in one sentence. Example: "Hero
moves left-to-right. Threat enters from top. Exit is off-camera right."

---

## Environment Plays (Kurosawa Principle)

Weather and environment are characters. Pick one environmental pressure per scene:

- Flickering fluorescent = decay, dread
- Rain on window = withheld grief
- Steam from kettle = suppressed anger
- Buzzing AC = dissociation
- Wet asphalt at night = guilt
- Tight corridor = walls closing in
- Mirror/glass = self-reckoning
- Overhead cold office light = judgment

## Three-Layer Storyboard Method

### Layer 1: Dramatic Beats (60–90 seconds)

```
0-5s     Hook         – Hero in tension, problem on screen
5-15s    Context      – Where, who, what's at stake
15-30s   Pressure     – Hero tries to hold control
30-45s   Crack        – Detail breaks hero's position
45-60s   Acceleration – Cuts shorten, breath tightens
60-75s   Impact       – Decision, break, confession, action
75-90s   Aftermath    – Silence or visual residue
```

Compress proportionally for 30s or 15s pieces. **Never skip Crack or Impact.**

### Layer 2: Shot Functions

- **Establish** — where we are
- **Power** — who controls now
- **Pressure** — what pushes down
- **Detail** — macro anchor (object, hand, eye, door)
- **Reaction** — face after event
- **Shift** — inner change made visible
- **Impact** — the decisive frame
- **Aftermath** — emptiness after action
- **Exit** — final image viewer carries out

### Layer 3: Editing Rhythm

Pattern structure (not random mincing):

```
long - shorter - shorter - pause - impact
```

**Rule:** the pause before impact matters more than speed of cuts. Without pause,
speed becomes visual meat grinder.

## Shot Card Template (14 Fields)

Fill every field. Missing fields reveal missing direction.

- **Shot ID** — 01, 02, 03…
- **Beat** — what changes in story
- **Emotion** — fear, shame, anger, guilt, resolve, relief
- **Frame** — wide / medium / close-up / macro insert
- **Composition** — center, edge, negative space, reflection, silhouette, foreground obstruction
- **Camera** — static, push-in, handheld, tracking, whip-pan
- **Movement reason** — why camera moves (answer "what changed?")
- **Action** — exact physical event
- **Eye trace** — where viewer's gaze lands in first 0.3s
- **Duration** — 0.5s, 1s, 3s…
- **Cut type** — match cut, smash cut, cut on action, J-cut, L-cut
- **Sound** — breath, bass hit, street noise, phone ring, silence
- **Light/color** — cold, contrast, flicker, shadow, palette
- **Production note** — prop, location, actor direction

## Rhythm Ladder (Drama Timing)

### Slow-burn drama
`4s, 4s, 3s, 2s, 1s, pause, 2s`

### Commercial product arc
`3s, 2s, 1.5s, 1s, 0.5s (product macro), 2s (hero shot)`

### Anxiety build
`2s, 1s, 1s, 0.5s, 0.5s, 0.3s, pause, 1s`

### Impact scene
`pause, 0.2s (flash), 2s (aftermath in stillness)`

**Always insert at least one pause before the biggest cut.**

## One-Anchor Principle

Commit to exactly five anchors:

1. One main emotion
2. One visual motif
3. One anchor object
4. One break
5. One final image

These five can be storyboarded and prompted into weight-bearing work rather than
stacked vague adjectives.

---

## Worked Example: Guilt (30 seconds)

### Five Anchors
- **Emotion:** guilt
- **Motif:** reflections in glass
- **Anchor object:** phone with unread message
- **Break:** he deletes it
- **Final image:** face ghosted on dark phone screen

### Layer 1: Beats
- 0–3s: phone buzzes on dark desk, face lit from below
- 3–10s: small office after hours, cold fluorescent, reflection in monitor
- 10–18s: stares at message, jaw tightens, hand hovers
- 18–22s: starts typing, stops, deletes character by character
- 22–26s: tight cuts — finger, screen, eye, breath, window reflection
- 26–28s: taps Delete Conversation, one tap, silence
- 28–30s: screen black, face remains reflected in dark glass

### Layer 2: Shot Functions
- Shot 03: Establish empty office
- Shot 05: Power — he alone, room looms
- Shot 07: Detail — phone close-up
- Shot 09: Reaction — jaw tightening
- Shot 11: Shift — hand hesitates
- Shot 13: Impact — thumb taps Delete
- Shot 14: Aftermath — dark screen, ghosted reflection

### Layer 3: Rhythm (final 10s)
- 2s: finger hovers
- 1s: face
- 0.5s: thumb
- 0.5s: confirmation prompt
- 1s: eyes
- 0.5s: thumb taps Delete
- 3s silence: screen black
- 1.5s: reflection on dark phone

## Dramaturgy Check (Before Sending)

Run this six-point verification:

1. ✓ Scene formula complete? (desire + obstacle + geometry + gaze + rhythm)
2. ✓ Every shot passes three-detail check? (environmental pressure + micro-action + sound/motif)
3. ✓ Every shot does one of three jobs? (emotion, action, pressure)
4. ✓ Motivated reason for every camera move?
5. ✓ Spatial geometry readable?
6. ✓ Five anchors named? (emotion, motif, object, break, final image)

**If any answer is no, fix before sending. Step 2 is most violated.**
